## Hero

**Headline:** Dinner, decided.

**Subhead:** Pantry looks at what's already in your fridge and plans the week's dinners from it.

**Intro paragraph:**

Somebody in your house has to decide what is for dinner, seven nights a week, and it is always you, at the worst hour of the day. Pantry looks at what is already in your fridge, from a photo of your groceries or a scan of the receipt, and suggests the week's dinners from it, with a shopping list for only the gaps. The chicken gets cooked before it turns, because you are reminded in time, and you stop paying twice for things you already had. You decide once, on Sunday, with a cup of tea. Then the fridge door is just a fridge door.

## Blade 1: Dinner, decided

You decide dinner once, on the weekend, for the whole week, and then you stop deciding. No more standing at the fridge at 5:40 with two hungry children and a blank mind. No more "I don't know, what do you want?" The question that has been yours by default every night of the week gets answered in one sitting, and the answer holds.

- A week of dinners, suggested at once. Decide on Sunday, not seven times at 5:40.
- A shopping list for only what the week needs and you don't have. The plan survives to Thursday.

## Blade 2: Nothing goes off

The chicken you bought on Saturday gets cooked, not found. So does the spinach, the half-tub of yoghurt, and the herbs you swore you would use. You stop throwing food and money into the bin at the end of the week, and you stop feeling bad about it, because the bin stays empty.

- Every dinner starts from what's already in your fridge. Saturday's chicken is Tuesday's dinner, not next week's bin.
- A reminder before something goes off, while there's still time to cook it.
- [Third proof line: needed from engineering. How many days ahead the reminder fires, or a real figure for food saved. A number here does more than any sentence.]

## Blade 3: Nothing to type

You never type in what you own. Every meal-planning app you have abandoned wanted an evening of data entry before it would help you, and you had no evening to give. Pantry asks for a photo. Your fridge is in the app without a keyboard.

- Photograph your groceries and Pantry knows what's in the fridge.
- Scan the receipt and the whole shop goes in at once.

## Notes for the builder

- Blade order is why-buy order and it matters: the decision is the biggest pain, so it goes first. Do not lead with the camera feature because it is the cleverest; it is third because it is the how, not the why.
- Keep the three why-buy headings exactly as written wherever they appear: app store listing, ads, emails. The house exists to give everyone the same three phrases.
- There is no testimonial section because there is no sourced quote. If a user has said "I hate deciding what's for dinner" in writing, put it between the hero and blade 1 with their first name, and consider using it as the headline.
- Nothing on this page says how long a dinner takes, whether the kids will eat it, whether a partner can see the plan, or how the app knows expiry dates. Those are not on the page because they are not in the intake. If any exist, add them to the house and they will show up here as third proof lines or a fourth blade.
- Ads, paid and social: one ad per why-buy, headline is the why-buy, body is the top row of its column, CTA "Photograph your fridge". Three ads, so you learn which reason the market answers to.
