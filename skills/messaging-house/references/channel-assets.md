# Channel copy from the messaging house

Read this before stage 6. Every channel is a derivation from the house (stage 4) and the why-buy copy and value proposition (stage 5). The test for any line on any channel: which cell of the house does it come from? No cell, no line.

## Four-blade homepage (the default work product)

This is the artefact the method was built to produce. Intro: the value proposition paragraph, or its first and last sentences as headline and subhead, with the whole paragraph beneath. Then four blades, one per why-buy, in column order. Each blade has the why-buy as its heading, the first paragraph of the why-buy copy as its body, and three or four house rows beneath as proof, phrased as short lines. Pricing, integrations, security and other parking-lot facts get their own strip after the blades. Specifications, if any, sit at the bottom and stay numeric.

Headline: under ten words, names the outcome, never the technology. Subhead: one sentence naming the product and the customer's biggest gain. Calls to action match the customer's next job (see a real invoice, start with one client, book a walkthrough), not the company's (learn more).

For software, a landing page for a single campaign uses the same structure with one blade promoted to the top: the why-buy the campaign is testing.

## Press release

Paragraph one: what is announced, for whom, and the value proposition's core claim in one sentence. Paragraph two: the two strongest why-buys, one sentence each with a proof row. Paragraph three: a quote that states the emotional job the product answers, in the executive's voice. Paragraph four: the remaining why-buys and availability. Boilerplate last. The why-buy copy supplies paragraphs two and four nearly verbatim.

## Paid ads and social

Each ad tests one why-buy. Headline is the outcome in the customer's words; body is the single strongest row from that column; call to action is the customer's next job. Produce a set of four, one per why-buy, so the team learns which reason the market responds to. Do not combine why-buys in one ad; it dilutes both the test and the message.

## Email

A launch sequence is one email on the value proposition, then one per why-buy in column order. Subject lines are the outcome. Body is the first paragraph of the why-buy copy, two or three rows as short lines, one call to action.

## Sales one-pager and deck

The one-pager is the house itself, formatted: value proposition on top, four columns beneath, pricing or specifications at the bottom. A deck gets a value proposition slide and one slide per why-buy (heading, three rows, one image). Objection handling comes from the pains list: for each top pain, the why-buy and rows that answer it.

## Naming and voice

Use the product name as supplied, once on first mention per asset and sparingly after. Keep why-buy names identical across channels; renaming them per channel breaks the shared language the house exists to create. When a brand voice guide exists, apply it at the sentence level and leave the structure alone.
